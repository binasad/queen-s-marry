Why Your Google Sign-In Breaks in Production
— and how SHA fingerprints actually work

Saad Bin Asad March 2026 8 min read

You tested Google Sign-In in debug mode — it worked perfectly. You uploaded your app to the Play Store. Now it's broken. No error message that makes sense. Just silent failure. This is one of the most common traps in Android development, and it comes down to one misunderstood concept: SHA fingerprints.

The Problem Nobody Explains Clearly

When developers set up Firebase or Google OAuth for their Android app, they're told to "add the SHA fingerprint." Simple enough. But there are actually two completely different SHA fingerprints in play when you use Google Play — and using the wrong one is what silently breaks authentication in production.

To understand why, you need to understand what happens to your APK between the moment you upload it and when a user downloads it.

Google Play re-signs your app with its own managed key before delivering it to users. Your original signature is gone.

Two Keys. Two SHAs. One Big Confusion.

When you enroll in Google Play App Signing (which is now mandatory for new apps), two separate cryptographic keys exist for your app:

⬆ Upload Key

The key you use to sign your AAB/APK before uploading to Play Console. Google uses this to verify you are the one uploading. After verification, Google strips this signature.

📦 App Signing Key

Google's own managed key that re-signs your app before delivery to users. This is the signature users' devices actually see. This is what Firebase and OAuth need to trust.

The SHA fingerprint that matters for Firebase, Google Maps, and OAuth is the one from the App Signing Key — not the Upload Key. Most developers add the Upload Key SHA and wonder why production auth is broken.

Where to Find the Correct SHA in Play Console

Here is exactly where to navigate:

1

Open Google Play Console and select your app

2

Left sidebar → Release → Setup → App integrity

3

In the App integrity screen, scroll to the "Play app signing" section and click on it — it's a clickable row, not just a label

4

You'll see two certificate sections. Copy the SHA-1 and SHA-256 from "App signing key certificate" — this is the one you need

5

Also note the "Upload key certificate" SHA-1 — you'll need this for local development

⚠ Common Mistake

The App integrity overview page (the one listing Play Integrity API, Automatic Protection, etc.) does not show the certificate fingerprints. You must click through to the App Signing sub-page to find them.

Where to Paste the SHAs

Firebase Console

→

Firebase Console → Project Settings → Your Apps → SHA certificate fingerprints → Add fingerprint

Add both fingerprints here:

  • SHA-1 from App signing key certificate (for production)
  • SHA-1 from Upload key certificate (for local debug builds)
  • SHA-256 from App signing key certificate (required for some Firebase features)

Google Cloud Console (OAuth)

→

Google Cloud Console → APIs & Services → Credentials → OAuth 2.0 Client ID (Android) → Edit → SHA-1 certificate fingerprint

fingerprint reference # Production (from App signing key certificate in Play Console) SHA-1 AB:CD:EF:12:34:... ← paste here into Firebase + OAuth SHA-256 12:AB:CD:EF:... ← paste here into Firebase # Local dev (from Upload key certificate in Play Console) SHA-1 FF:AA:BB:CC:... ← also add to Firebase for debug builds

Why Your Debug Build Works But Production Doesn't

When you run your app locally via Android Studio, it's signed with your local debug keystore (found at ~/.android/debug.keystore). Firebase recognizes this because you added the debug SHA at setup time.

But when a user downloads your app from the Play Store, it's signed by Google's App Signing Key — a completely different fingerprint. Firebase and Google's OAuth service see an unknown signature and reject authentication silently.

This is why the fix has nothing to do with your code. You haven't done anything wrong in the app itself. The missing piece is simply registering Google's signing key SHA with Firebase and OAuth.

The Complete Fix — Step by Step

1

Go to Play Console → App integrity → Play app signing and copy both the App signing key SHA-1 and SHA-256

2

Open Firebase Console → Project Settings → Your App and add the SHA-1 and SHA-256. Download the updated google-services.json

3

Replace the old google-services.json in your project's app/ directory

4

If using Google Sign-In via OAuth, update your OAuth 2.0 Android Client ID in Google Cloud Console with the same SHA-1

5

Build a new release AAB, upload to Play Console, and test via Internal Testing track before pushing to production

💡 Pro Tip

Changes to Firebase SHA fingerprints take effect immediately — you don't need to re-publish your app. But if you updated OAuth credentials, allow a few minutes for Google's servers to propagate the change.

Summary

The confusion is understandable — Google Play Console's interface buries the signing certificate details, and the distinction between "upload key" and "app signing key" is rarely explained up front. But once you understand the flow, the fix is straightforward:

  • Google Play re-signs your app before delivery — your upload key SHA is not what users' devices see
  • The correct SHA lives in Play Console → App integrity → Play app signing → App signing key certificate
  • Add this SHA (not your upload key SHA) to Firebase and Google Cloud OAuth
  • Add both SHAs to Firebase so both debug and production builds work
  • Download the updated google-services.json after adding new fingerprints

This single misunderstanding wastes hours for developers every day. Now you know exactly where to look and what to do.
